03 / FRAGMENTS FROM THE ARCHIVE

What the symbols remember

Collected folklore. No origin has ever been verified.

FRAGMENT 01
⌁

A language with no birthplace

The String Alphabet has an unknown origin. Its age remains a mystery, though rumours claim it was an ancient language used to express gratitude, affection, resentment, or hatred toward a particular person, place, or idea.

It appears most often at the end of letters, messages, and passages—as if the writer has said everything they can in ordinary language, then added the truth they could not bring themselves to write.

FRAGMENT 02
♡

A birthday that says more

Some believe a person's birthday, when written in String, can resemble “I love you.” The date is not simply a date: it can stand for the moment a person entered your world—and the quiet gratitude that they did.

FRAGMENT 03
✳

Ordinary words, hidden vows

A favourite colour may secretly mean “You're my favourite person.” “Thank you” can mean “I will remember what you did for me.” In this language, the literal message is only the surface; the relationship between sender and reader supplies the deeper meaning.

FRAGMENT 04
⌖

Affection has a shadow

The same phrase can carry opposite meanings. “Goodbye” may mean “I'll find you again,” or it may be a final warning. “I miss you” can be tenderness—or an admission that someone has become impossible to forget.

FRAGMENT 05
〰

Meaning depends on who reads it

String is said to have no fixed dictionary for its emotional meanings. A simple “blue” could mean calm, distance, loyalty, or “You're my favourite person,” depending on the sender's private associations. A number may be a date, a memory, a promise, or a sentence compressed into a code.

That is why two people can translate the same message and come away with different truths. The glyphs preserve the words; the heart supplies the meaning.

FRAGMENT 06
☾

Why it disappeared

One legend says it was passed between trusted people in margins, folded notes, and the final lines of letters. Another says it was never a language at all, but a ritual shared by people who understood one another. No one knows which story came first.
